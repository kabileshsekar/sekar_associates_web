Overview

Casa Botanica was born from a desire to dissolve the boundary between interior and exterior. The brief asked for a home that felt simultaneously open to the tropical landscape and intimate enough for a family of four.

We responded with a layered sequence of spaces — each room a carefully composed frame for the garden beyond, connected by a choreography of light that shifts throughout the day.

Living room, facing the garden
Concept

The material palette was assembled over eighteen months of sourcing — from hand-fired ceramic tiles imported from Portugal to solid Brazilian walnut harvested from certified forests in Mato Grosso.

Every surface was chosen for its ability to age well, to accumulate the memory of the people who inhabit it. The floors, for instance, will darken over decades, a living record of the life lived here.

"The best rooms are the ones you never want to leave — not because they hold you, but because they feel completely yours."
— Lead Designer, Sekar Associates
01 / Master Suite

A room designed for

The master suite occupies the entire eastern wing, opening onto a private courtyard garden accessible only from within. Blackout linen panels filter the morning light to a soft amber glow, while the en-suite bath is carved from a single slab of Calacatta marble.

02 / Kitchen

Where the family

At the heart of the home, the kitchen was designed as a living room unto itself. A twelve-metre island in honed Nero Marquina marble anchors the space, surrounded by custom joinery in smoked oak with unlacquered brass hardware that will develop its own patina over time.
